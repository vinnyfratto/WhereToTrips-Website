# Holibob and Viator Recreate Nuitée for Experiences

No single partner gives WhereTo Trips the whole Nuitée deal across tours, events and cars: one API, the partner as merchant of record, refunds handled by the partner, a round-the-clock support desk and a commission paid after travel. Nuitée itself sells none of the three. For tours, activities and excursions, two partners come close enough to build on. **Holibob** is a UK aggregator that claims **500,000+ bookable products** (tours, attractions, live events and dining) from 45+ suppliers. It documents a Stripe checkout in which **Holibob is the merchant of record**, the same pattern Nuitée uses for flights, and it offers net-rate or commission terms. **Viator's "Full + Booking" tier** lets the app book natively while **Viator stays merchant of record and handles customer service, cancellations and refunds**. It typically pays about 8%, once Viator approves and certifies the integration. The recommended path has three steps. Open Holibob's free sandbox now. Apply for Viator Full + Booking the same week. Make Holibob the primary partner only if it confirms in writing that it owns traveler support and refunds; if it will not, Viator leads. Concert and pro-sports tickets have no Nuitée-style option open to a startup, because Ticketmaster limits its purchase API to official distribution partners, so those stay as tracked links for now. Cars, the secondary goal, can launch within weeks through Discover Cars' affiliate program. Meanwhile WhereTo can negotiate native booking with Expedia's Rapid Car API (Expedia is merchant of record on pay-now rentals) or with Duffel Cars. One limitation applies throughout: vendor pages could not be fetched directly. These findings rest on search-result summaries of those pages, plus GetYourGuide's public GitHub API spec, and most commission rates are unpublished.

## Nuitée's arrangement sets a bar most partners miss

WhereTo's own partner page sets the standard. Nuitée is the **booking agent and merchant of record**. Card details go straight to its PCI-compliant processor, and it returns the money when a booking is refunded. Its 24/7 flight desk handles airline schedule changes, and travelers contact Nuitée directly about changes and problems. WhereTo adds no booking fee and is **paid a commission after travel** ([WhereTo Trips: Nuitée partner page](../src/about/nuitee.md)). LiteAPI's flight documentation shows how this works underneath. Partners embed Nuitée's Stripe SDK so the card is charged under Nuitée's company. Support runs one of two ways: "First Line," where travelers contact Nuitée, or "B2B-Relayed," where the partner handles support up front and passes cases on to Nuitée ([LiteAPI: Flights support & billing model](https://docs.liteapi.travel/docs/flights-support-billing-model)). Nothing in LiteAPI's documentation, its Q1 2026 product update or its 2026 partnerships covers cars, transfers, activities or events. Its roadmap is AI tooling and flights ([LiteAPI docs](https://docs.liteapi.travel/reference/getting-started-1); [Nuitée Q1 2026 update](https://nuitee.com/insights/liteapi-product-updates-q1-2026); [liteapi.travel/about](https://liteapi.travel/about)). WhereTo needs a second partner, and probably a third.

Every candidate falls into one of three business models, and the model matters more than the brand. **Affiliate:** WhereTo shows the product and hands the customer to the partner's website or app. The partner does everything else, and WhereTo gets a percentage, usually 1–10%, if the customer buys within a tracking window (the "cookie"). **Merchant-of-record booking API** (the Nuitée model): the customer books inside WhereTo's app, but the partner's name is on the card charge. The partner deals with refunds, chargebacks (disputed card charges) and traveler support, and WhereTo earns a commission. **Net-rate reseller** (sometimes called "merchant" or "wholesale"): the partner sells to WhereTo at a wholesale price, WhereTo sets the retail price, takes the payment and becomes the seller. WhereTo then owns fraud, refunds, chargebacks and the support desk, and often has to post a deposit or credit line. Net rates pay more, but they would turn WhereTo into a different kind of company from the one its partner page describes. They would also likely raise seller-of-travel registration questions that WhereTo's lawyers should check before taking that route.

The middle model is scarce, and getting scarcer. GetYourGuide stopped accepting card and PayPal payments through its Partner API in **November 2024** and removed them from the spec in **April 2025**. It now tells partners to "redirect the user to the GetYourGuide website," and the only native checkout left is an invoiced method called "MasterBill" ([GYG commit, Nov 2024](https://github.com/getyourguide/partner-api-spec/commit/1e54d0d1d59feb37a6eadf5d743f653fef888aaa); [GYG commit, Apr 2025](https://github.com/getyourguide/partner-api-spec/commit/7b5c372a75a49027ce9add92847029876937b020)). Amadeus closed its self-service portal on **17 July 2026**, and that portal had included a Tours & Activities API ([PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/amadeus-shut-down-self-service-apis-portal-developers)).

The booking systems most tour operators run on (Bókun, Rezdy, Ventrata, FareHarbor) and the open OCTO technical standard do not fill the gap either. On Bókun's marketplace, payments happen outside the platform and each operator invoices the reseller. OCTO issues API keys per reseller–operator pair, so every supplier means another contract ([Bókun docs](https://docs.bokun.io/docs/marketplace-pro/marketplace/how-does-the-bokun-marketplace-work-for-suppliers-pro); [Zaui OCTO docs](https://docs.zaui.com/octo)). These are tools for signing a few top operators directly later, not a source of launch inventory.

## Holibob and Viator are the experience partners that meet it

### Holibob matches Nuitée's setup most closely

Holibob's partner documentation tells integrators to build a Stripe payment flow "so that Holibob acts as the merchant of record." The app requests a Stripe payment session (a "PaymentIntent") through Holibob's GraphQL API and shows the card form itself ([Holibob API payment flow](https://partner.documentation.holibob.tech/general/Working-version/the-holibob-api-payment-flow)). That is the same pattern as Nuitée's flight checkout. Holibob offers **net rates or commission sharing** ([holibob.tech](https://holibob.tech/)). Its finance documentation covers Apple Pay, Google Pay and payment in multiple currencies ([Holibob finance docs](https://docs.holibob.tech/docs/platform-guide/finance)), and its API comes with a sandbox ([Holibob GraphQL overview](https://docs.holibob.tech/docs/graphql/overview)).

In September 2026 Holibob released a **free SDK**: ready-made search, product pages and checkout, built as web components with a small server component. It connects to **500,000+ products from 45+ API partners plus direct contracts, including live events and dining**. Holibob claims it cuts integration time from about six months to three days ([Breaking Travel News](https://www.breakingtravelnews.com/news/article/holibob-launches-sdk-that-cuts-experience-booking-integration-from-six-mont/)). The sandbox charges no payments and contacts no suppliers; going live requires a signed commercial agreement ([ShortTermRentalz](https://shorttermrentalz.com/news/holibob-launches-toolkit-experience-bookings/)). Holibob also supplies Google Things to Do and launched conversational booking in June 2026, which suits WhereTo's AI-led Vibe Engine ([PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/news/technology/holibob-conversational-booking-travel-experiences)).

The gaps are real, and the biggest one is the traveler. Holibob's platform guide lists "Merchant Of Record," "Partner Support" and "Supply Contracting" as services, but only its table of contents was visible, so **it is unconfirmed whether Holibob answers travelers directly, around the clock, the way Nuitée does** ([Holibob platform guide](https://docs.holibob.tech/docs/platform-guide/overview)). Commission levels, refund liability and the supplier list are not public either. Nobody knows whether its 500,000 products repackage Viator or GetYourGuide inventory; if they do, WhereTo would see duplicate listings and pay stacked margins. The SDK is web-based, so a native app would either show it in an embedded web view or build native screens on the API and complete the payment with Stripe's own iOS and Android libraries, which Holibob still needs to confirm it supports. Holibob is also a much smaller company than Nuitée. Its last disclosed raise was a **$12M Series A in 2022**, plus an undisclosed 2024 investment ([PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/news/technology/holibob-conversational-booking-travel-experiences)), and its named partners (Kayak, Amadeus, Secret Escapes) come from an older list ([Holibob Series A post](https://www.holibob.tech/blog/holibob-secures-12m-series-a-investment-to-transform-curation-distribution-and-commerce-in-tours-and-activities?1b4b4d01_page=2)).

### Viator has the best-documented support model

Viator publishes four access levels: **Basic** affiliate access, available instantly; **Full** access, which adds content and availability; **Full + Booking**, which adds booking, hold and cancellation; and **Merchant**, which adds everything ([Viator: levels of access](https://partnerresources.viator.com/travel-commerce/levels-of-access/)). Under Full + Booking, customers book inside the partner's app using Viator's payment tools, while **Viator remains merchant of record and handles customer service** ([Viator affiliate program](https://partnerresources.viator.com/travel-commerce/affiliate/)). The app holds the booking, loads Viator's embedded payment form, then confirms. Viator handles communication about bookings, cancellations and refunds, and customers cancel through Viator support ([Viator technical guide](https://partnerresources.viator.com/travel-commerce/technical-guide/)). Of everything in this research, this is the closest documented match to Nuitée's "deal with us directly" arrangement.

Viator is also the largest single marketplace: **over 400,000 experiences** by its own count, and 300,000+ on its current app listing ([Viator About](https://www.viator.com/support/about); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.viator.mobile.android&hl=en)). It already powers Revolut's in-app Experiences and Capital One Travel's activities ([Revolut legal](https://www.revolut.com/en-NZ/legal/experiences/); [Capital One](https://www.capitalone.com/learn-grow/more-than-money/things-to-do-in-san-francisco)).

The trade-offs are approval, the payment screen and supply. Full + Booking needs Viator's authorization and a certification of the build ([Viator certification](https://partnerresources.viator.com/travel-commerce/certification/)), and no timeline or traffic minimum was found. Payment happens in an embedded web form, so in a native app it would sit inside a web view; no native SDK or Apple Pay support was confirmed. Viator's own page promises only "a standard commission rate." Affiliate directories consistently list **8% with a 30-day cookie**, though one US listing shows 3.2% and one Travelpayouts article says 4% ([Lasso: Viator](https://getlasso.co/affiliate/viator/); [Lasso: Viator US](https://getlasso.co/affiliate/viator-us/); [Travelpayouts](https://www.travelpayouts.com/blog/best-concert-event-ticket-affiliate-program/)). Inventory is Viator-only, with little concert or sports supply. Later, the Merchant tier lets WhereTo add its own markup over a wholesale price, but it makes WhereTo the merchant of record and requires a deposit sized to expected sales ([Viator merchant](https://partnerresources.viator.com/travel-commerce/merchant/); [Viator pricing](https://partnerresources.viator.com/travel-commerce/merchant/pricing/)). At 8%, a $50–$150 tour earns roughly $4–$12 per booking, so experiences mainly add trip value and keep users coming back; on their own they are a modest revenue line.

### Every other option fails at least one test

**TUI Musement** is a credible third option with the right model. Its affiliate API routes payment through Musement's Stripe checkout with **Musement as merchant of record**, and a merchant API is available later ([Musement Partner API](https://partner-api.musement.com/api/getting-started); [Musement payment flow](https://partner-api.musement.com/api/booking-flow/payment)). It had **88,000+ experiences and 125,000+ transfer routes** in 2023 ([TUI Group](https://tuigroup.com/en-en/media/press-releases/2023/2023-10-06-tui-driving-further-growth-in-tours-and-activities)). It powers airline partners such as Jet2 (February 2026) and provides customer support in its Volaris deal ([Jet2](https://www.jet2.com/news/2026/02/Jet2_partners_with_Musement_to_offer_customers_thousands_of_in_destination_experiences); [Musement/Volaris](https://partner.musement.com/tui-continues-global-growth-in-tours-activities-with-partnership-with-leading-mexican-airline-volaris)). Its commission is unclear: one secondhand report says it shares "50% from the own income" ([Travelpayouts Q&A](https://www.travelpayouts.com/blog/qa-from-the-musement-webinar/)). Its supply leans toward Europe.

**GetYourGuide** has the best public documentation; it was the only source read directly for this report. It offers **75,000+ activities** with reviews, itineraries and mobile-ticket flags, and pays up to 8% ([GYG wiki](https://github.com/getyourguide/partner-api-spec/wiki); [GYG creator support](https://partner.getyourguide.support/hc/en-us/articles/23082933149981)). But native checkout now needs MasterBill access, which the spec never defines. A 2024 support article also set **300 bookings a month** as the bar for basic data access ([GYG support](https://partner.getyourguide.support/hc/en-us/articles/13981133907613-API-integration-and-requirements)). For WhereTo, GetYourGuide is a redirect partner.

**Tiqets** only opens its Booking API at **about 200 orders a month** and pays a share of its gross margin ([Tiqets API program](https://www.tiqets.com/en/partner-program/api-program/); [Tiqets commission model](https://partners.tiqets.com/en_us/commission-model-BkgPnGdu3)). Expedia now owns Tiqets, and Tiqets supplies Expedia's new **Rapid Activities API**. That API is in beta for selected partners, with full launch planned for early 2027 ([Travel PR News](https://travelprnews.com/travel-press-releases-tagged-with/full-trip-platform/); [Expedia IR, Oct 2026](https://ir.expediagroup.com/news-and-events/news/news-details/2026/Expedia-Group-B2B-Announces-Product-Milestones-to-Expand-Its-Full-Trip-Platform--/default.aspx)). Chase Travel has already moved its activities from Viator to Expedia ([The Points Guy](https://thepointsguy.com/guide/using-chase-ultimate-rewards-points-for-activities)).

**Headout's** in-app "API Partner" tier is effectively a net-rate deal. Partners see a wholesale price, and when a booking is cancelled, Headout credits the partner's prepaid account and the partner refunds the customer. Headout refunds customers directly only in its affiliate tier ([Headout partner docs](https://partner.headout.com/docs)).

**Hotelbeds** makes its money on the gap between what it pays suppliers and what it charges resellers. Its activities API would therefore make WhereTo the seller, and its last public count was **18,000 activities (2021)** ([Wikipedia: HBX Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBX_Group); [TravelDailyNews](https://www.traveldailynews.com/post/hotelbeds-further-expands-experiences-distribution-reach-through-nemo-group-partnership)). Its parent HBX agreed in May 2026 to buy Bridgify for €3M upfront plus performance payments. Bridgify claims 1M+ experiences and targets fintechs and super-apps, but has no public API documentation ([PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/news/technology/hbx-group-acquires-bridgify)).

**Klook's** public API is for suppliers loading inventory onto Klook; reseller APIs are reserved for larger partners ([Klook OpenAPI](https://klook.gitbook.io/openapi); [Klook Affiliate](https://affiliate.klook.com/home)). **Civitatis** pays 8–10% but requires a travel website ([Civitatis affiliates](https://www.civitatis.com/en/affiliates/); [Civitatis FAQ](https://www.civitatis.com/en/affiliates/landing-frequently-asked-questions/)).

## Concert and sports tickets stay as tracked links for now

Ticketmaster is the obvious data source but the wrong booking partner. Its Discovery API is free, covers events in more than 25 markets, and allows 5,000 calls a day by default. Once WhereTo links an Impact affiliate account, tracking is added to every event link automatically ([Ticketmaster Discovery API](https://developer.ticketmaster.com/products-and-docs/apis/discovery-api/v2/); [Ticketmaster affiliate sign-up](https://developer.ticketmaster.com/partners/distribution-partners/affiliate-sign-up/)). Its Partner API, which can reserve and buy tickets, is "not an open API." It is limited to companies with existing official distribution relationships ([Ticketmaster Partner API](https://developer.ticketmaster.com/products-and-docs/apis/partner/)). Affiliate pay is thin: about **1% in the UK and $0.30 per ticket in Australia**, and no official US rate was found ([Travelpayouts: Ticketmaster](https://www.travelpayouts.com/en/offers/ticketmaster-affiliate-program)). Approval favors partners who bring "distinct and unique audiences" ([Ticketmaster FAQ](https://developer.ticketmaster.com/support/faq/)).

SeatGeek's free API is discovery-only and can be withdrawn without notice. Its affiliate terms also conflict: directories list 2–5% on Impact, while its partner license says SeatGeek "is not required to make any payments to you" ([SeatGeek API terms](https://seatgeek.com/api-terms); [SeatGeek partner terms](https://seatgeek.com/partner-terms)). StubHub pays 4% (0.8% on baseball) through the Partnerize network with a 30-day cookie, although other listings range from 3.2% to 9% ([Partnerize](https://partnerize.com/resources/blog/stubhub-affiliate-program-spotlight-and-sign-up-information); [StubHub affiliates](https://stubhub.com/affiliates)).

Only two routes let WhereTo sell concert and sports tickets inside its own app while someone else carries the payment risk, and both sell resale tickets. In **Ticket Evolution's white-label affiliate tier**, Ticket Evolution handles "the credit card transaction and all fraud checks" plus customer service, and its hosted checkout supports Apple Pay and Google Pay ([Ticket Evolution access](https://ticketevolution.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/API/pages/342229112); [Hosted Checkout](https://ticketevolution.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/API/pages/3798958097/Hosted+Checkout)). **TicketNetwork Private Label** runs checkout, payments, ticket delivery and customer support behind the partner's brand, and TicketNetwork's standard affiliate program pays the highest rate found anywhere in this research, **12.5–14.5%** ([TicketNetwork affiliate](https://www.ticketnetwork.com/en/affiliate-program); [TicketNetwork LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/ticketnetwork)). Both go through a sales team and neither publishes terms. Resale pricing also brings trust and legal exposure. Since **12 May 2025**, the FTC's fee rule has required the full price, fees included, wherever a live-event ticket price is shown, and it applies to third-party platforms. StubHub agreed to pay **$10M** back to consumers in April 2026 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/05/ftc-bans-hidden-fees-for-live-events-and-short-term-rentals-effective-may-12/); [Ohio AG](https://ohioattorneygeneral.gov/Media/Newsletters/Consumer-Advocate/August-2025/Know-your-rights-FTC-s-rule-on-unfair-or-deceptive); [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/04/stubhub-refunding-10-million-fees-consumers-after-deceptive-ticket-pricing)). Any ticket price WhereTo shows from these feeds should include mandatory fees.

Experience partners serve the travel-friendly kinds of events better: shows, classical and candlelit concerts, and Broadway. Holibob lists live events in its catalog and signed Classictic for classical concerts, opera and ballet in Europe. Coverage of that deal put Holibob's catalog at 100,000 experiences, an older figure than today's 500,000 ([Travolution](https://www.travolution.com/news/holibob-curates-experiences-for-music-and-culture-lovers-with-classictic-partnership/)). Headout carries Broadway ([Headout partner docs](https://partner.headout.com/docs)). Fever bought the ticketing app DICE in June 2025. It offers a free reseller platform with wholesale prices on **200,000+ experiences in 500+ cities**, although its affiliate cookie lasts just one day ([Fever distributors](https://business.feverup.com/en/partner-programs/experience-distribution-platform/); [Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2025/06/05/ticketer-dice-acquired-by-events-discovery-platform-fever/); [FlexOffers: Fever](https://www.flexoffers.com/affiliate-programs/fever-up-affiliate-program)). Eventbrite removed public event search in 2020, so it is no help for finding events at a destination ([GitHub issue quoting Eventbrite](https://github.com/Automattic/eventbrite-api/issues/83)).

The long-term wild card is the April 2026 jury verdict that Live Nation and Ticketmaster illegally monopolized primary ticketing. Remedies such as requiring an open platform are under discussion, but the penalties phase runs into 2027 ([NY AG](https://ag.ny.gov/press-release/2026/attorney-general-james-and-coalition-states-win-trial-against-live-nation-and); [Courthouse News](https://courthousenews.com/penalties-phase-of-live-nation-ticket-monopoly-trial-will-stretch-into-2027/)).

## Cars offer native merchant-of-record options, all gated or in beta

**Expedia's Rapid Car API** is the closest car equivalent to LiteAPI. It covers availability, prices and extras, payment, booking, retrieval and cancellation ([Expedia Rapid Car](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/cars)). Each offer says who the merchant of record is. **Pay-now rentals are charged by Expedia; pay-at-counter rentals are charged by the rental company.** Add-ons such as child seats are always paid at the counter, so the app must show clearly what is due now and what is due at pickup ([Rapid Car launch requirements](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/setup/launch-requirements/car-launch-reqs?locale=ko_KR)). Supply covers **110+ brands in 190 countries and 45,000+ pickup vendors** ([Expedia Partner: Rapid Car](https://partner.expediagroup.com/en-us/landing-pages/rapid-car-api-for-scalable-car-rental-bookings)). It is in beta with selected partners, and Expedia's own pages disagree on when it fully launches: "early 2027" on the US page, "early 2026" on the Australian page ([Expedia AU](https://partner.expediagroup.com/en-au/landing-pages/rapid-car-api-for-scalable-car-rental-bookings)).

**Booking.com's Demand API** offers the same model with more friction. Its stable car flow sends travelers to Booking.com to finish the booking. Booking inside the partner's app needs the beta Orders API. Booking.com takes the payment, and by default cancellation is free until 48 hours before pickup ([Booking.com car rentals](https://developers.booking.com/demand/docs/cars/overview); [car tutorial v3.2](https://developers.booking.com/demand/docs/cars/3.2/cars-tutorial); [payments](https://developers.booking.com/demand/docs/payments/overview); [cancellation policies](https://developers.booking.com/demand/docs/orders-api/cancellation-policies)). Access requires Managed Affiliate Partner status and a signed contract, and cars cannot be tested in the sandbox ([prerequisites](https://developers.booking.com/demand/docs/getting-started/prerequisites); [sandbox](https://developers.booking.com/demand/docs/getting-started/sandbox)). A secondary source reports that Booking.com's partner terms ban using an AI system to carry out the agreement without written approval ([Vorp Labs](https://vorplabs.com/agent-tools/booking-demand-api)). That clause was not checked against Booking.com's own text. Still, for an app built around an AI Vibe Engine, it is a reason to choose another partner unless Booking.com approves the use in writing.

**Duffel Cars** is the most startup-friendly native option. It offers 40 providers, including Avis, Sixt, Enterprise, Hertz and Europcar, across **40,000+ locations**, through the same API as Duffel's flights and hotels ([Duffel Cars](https://duffel.com/blog/introducing-duffel-cars)). Robinhood's new travel product keeps "the entire search, comparison, and payment process inside the Robinhood app" using Duffel ([FF News](https://ffnews.com/news/embargoed-robinhood-taps-duffel-to-power-its-travel-platform-104667f3)). About 85% of Duffel's volume is in the US ([BusinessCloud](https://businesscloud.co.uk/news/duffel-hits-900m-run-rate-and-launches-cars-vertical/)). The key term is still missing: no source confirms whether Duffel is merchant of record for car bookings, or who supports those travelers.

**Discover Cars** is the strongest affiliate program. It pays **70% of its profit plus 30% on its Full Coverage insurance, about $20 per booking on average, with a 365-day cookie** ([Discover Cars affiliate](https://www.discovercars.com/affiliate)). It covers 1,000+ suppliers at 50,000+ locations in 164 countries. Its B4B program for business partners adds a full API with booking and payment. In December 2025 the company said it was "rolling out" those booking and payment features to partners ([Discover Cars B4B](https://pages.discovercars.com/b4b); [Future Travel Experience](https://www.futuretravelexperience.com/2025/12/how-discovercars-com-is-revolutionising-car-rental-distribution-with-a-b4b-approach/)).

**CarTrawler** is the car platform most like Nuitée in structure, and the hardest to reach. Behind airline brands, its subsidiary handles payment, vouchers, cancellations, refunds and complaints ([CarTrawler T&Cs](https://mobility.cartrawler.com/flydubai/en-GB/terms-and-conditions?clientId=526782)), and it provides 24/7 multilingual support under the partner's name ([Genesys case study](https://www.genesys.com/customer-stories/cartrawler)). But it has no self-service sign-up ([apis.io](https://apis.io/providers/cartrawler/)), and reviews of Ryanair's car hire, which CarTrawler runs, describe prepaid money not refunded after disputes at the rental desk ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/car-hire.ryanair.com)).

**The research notes conflict on whether Expedia owns CarTrawler yet.** Expedia announced the deal on 19–20 May 2026 and expected it to close in the second half of 2026 ([BusinessWire](https://secure.businesswire.com/news/home/20260519556732/en/Expedia-Group-announces-agreement-to-acquire-CarTrawler-advancing-strategy-to-build-the-most-complete-B2B-travel-platform)). The car-rental research then found an Expedia partner blog post, dated 1 September 2026 in search results, reporting the deal complete ([Expedia Partner blog](https://partner.expediagroup.com/en-us/resources/blog/expedia-group-completes-cartrawler-acquisition)), while the platform research found no closing report as of October 2026. The price is disputed too: about $350M according to Skift, versus undisclosed with industry speculation above $450M according to the Irish Times ([Skift](https://skift.com/2026/05/17/expedia-350-million-cartrawler-acquisition-scoop/); [Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/2026/05/20/cartrawler-acquired-by-us-travel-group-expedia/)). So is its size: one source counts 1,700+ providers, while Expedia's release counts 550+ car suppliers plus 500+ mobility providers ([TravelDailyNews](https://www.traveldailynews.com/aviation/km-malta-airlines-adds-car-rental-booking-through-partnership-with-cartrawler/); [BusinessWire](https://secure.businesswire.com/news/home/20260519556732/en/Expedia-Group-announces-agreement-to-acquire-CarTrawler-advancing-strategy-to-build-the-most-complete-B2B-travel-platform)). Either way, WhereTo will probably reach CarTrawler's supply through Expedia Rapid eventually, not through a direct deal.

The remaining options are fallbacks. Priceline's car API, reportedly built on Rentalcars content, expects about five bookings a day ([AltexSoft](https://www.altexsoft.com/blog/car-rental-apis-integrations-with-gdss-otas-and-tech-providers/); [Supergood](https://supergood.ai/api-report-card/priceline)). The Rentalcars/Booking.com affiliate program pays about 6%, confirmed only after the car is returned ([Travelpayouts: Rentalcars](https://www.travelpayouts.com/blog/earn-on-car-rentals-with-rentalcars/)), and Expedia's affiliate program pays about 2% on cars with a 7-day cookie ([Lasso: Expedia](https://getlasso.co/affiliate/expedia/)). The airline-style booking systems known as GDSs earn about 5% on cars but require travel-agency accreditation ([Hertz travel agent policy](https://www.hertz.com/rentacar/misc/index.jsp?targetPage=TA_commissionpolicy.jsp)), and Turo removed outside API access in 2023 ([CarSync](https://carsyncio.medium.com)).

For airport transfers, which pair naturally with flights, Mozio offers an API, a white-label site or a widget across 3,500+ airports. It charges no fees and lets partners set their own markup ([Mozio business partners](http://www.mozio.com/business-partners); [Mozio travel agents](http://www.mozio.com/travel-agents)). Who acts as merchant of record for Mozio bookings was not found.

## A staged plan, with written answers before anything is signed

Start everything that is free and quick this month. Open Holibob's sandbox and build native screens against its API. Take Viator's instant Basic access to build discovery screens, and request Full + Booking right away. For cars, join Discover Cars' affiliate program and open it in an in-app browser, so the user stays close to the app while Discover Cars handles payment and support. For concerts and games, register for Ticketmaster's Discovery API and Impact affiliate program and show events at the destination as tracked links. The live app, with its flight and hotel bookings and its Nuitée relationship, is the credential to present in every application; the Tiqets, GetYourGuide and Priceline thresholds show that partners reward existing booking volume.

Then choose the main experience partner on evidence, not marketing. Holibob should confirm in writing who answers travelers and when, who issues refunds and absorbs chargebacks, its commission or net-rate terms and when they are paid (Nuitée pays after travel), how deep its US inventory is and where it comes from, whether its payment can be completed with Stripe's native mobile tools including Apple Pay, and any minimum volumes. Viator should state its Full + Booking timeline and requirements, and whether its payment form supports Apple Pay.

If Holibob matches Nuitée on support and refunds, it leads, because one contract covers tours, attractions, live events and dining; Viator then adds depth and a backup. If Holibob will not own the traveler relationship, Viator leads, and Holibob or Musement becomes the second source. For cars, open talks with Expedia (Rapid Car beta) and Duffel at the same time, and pick whichever first confirms it will be merchant of record and handle support. Keep Discover Cars running until then.

WhereTo should also check its own Nuitée agreement for exclusivity clauses. LiteAPI markets "zero contracts," but Nuitée signs exclusive deals with large partners such as Grab ([AnchorTerminal](https://www.anchorterminal.com/compare/booking-demand-api-vs-liteapi); [Nuitée](https://nuitee.com/)). Travelopro is excluded, as already decided.

Several other conflicts in the evidence should be settled in those conversations rather than assumed, alongside the CarTrawler closing, price and size disputes and the region-by-region Rapid Car launch dates covered above. Duffel Cars' launch is dated April 2026 in one summary and September 2026 in another, although it is live with Robinhood either way ([Duffel blog](https://duffel.com/blog)). One low-authority roundup says Holibob mainly connects travel brands to their existing suppliers rather than pooling supply itself, which contradicts Holibob's own 500,000-product claim ([Vocal.media](https://vocal.media/01/travel-experiences-distribution-platforms-top-travel-ap-is-and-white-label-solutions)). Viator's commission ranges from 3.2% to 8% depending on the listing, Headout's affiliate rate from 1% to 8% ([LinkClicky](https://linkclicky.com/affiliate-program/headout/); [FlexOffers](https://www.flexoffers.com/affiliate-programs/headout-inc-affiliate-program/)), and Hotelbeds' activity and transfer counts vary by source and year. Most of these numbers come from affiliate directories, not contracts, and the real rates will be whatever each partner offers WhereTo.

## Conclusion

What is scarce in this market is not inventory but the middle model: booking inside the app while the partner stays the seller. Big catalogs are everywhere. But GetYourGuide has already taken that option away from most partners, and Tiqets and Headout either ration it or restructure it so the partner carries refunds. Only Holibob and Viator offer it openly to a company of WhereTo's size. That argues for locking in terms now, while WhereTo's flight-and-hotel volume counts in its favor and Holibob is courting developers with a free SDK. It also argues for short contracts that let WhereTo take its data and leave cleanly, because consolidation keeps reshaping the field. HBX agreed to buy Bridgify, Travel Curious bought Redeam ([Travolution](https://www.travolution.com/news/travel-sectors/tours-and-activities/travel-curious-acquires-redeam-relocates-its-global-hq/)), and Expedia now owns Tiqets and has bought, or is about to close on, CarTrawler.

Expedia is the company to watch. By 2027 it may offer cars, Tiqets-backed activities and CarTrawler ground transport through one Rapid API. That would make it the closest multi-category match to what Nuitée does for flights and hotels, and it could replace two or three of the integrations recommended here. WhereTo's AI positioning cuts both ways in that choice. Holibob, Duffel and Expedia are building for conversational and AI-driven booking, while Booking.com's terms reportedly treat AI use as something that needs permission. The best partner for the Vibe Engine is one that lets the engine actually book.
